**Biological sex is more complex than a simple binary.**  
&nbsp;  
Approximately 1 in 4,500 people are born intersex—with variations in chromosomes, hormones, reproductive organs, or genitalia that don't fit typical male/female classifications. This is about as common as having red hair. Sex involves multiple factors: chromosomes, hormones, internal reproductive organs, external genitalia, and secondary sex characteristics—and these don't always align in the "typical" ways. Even among people who aren't intersex, there's significant natural variation in hormone levels and other sex characteristics. Modern biology recognizes sex as a spectrum of characteristics rather than two rigid categories. This biological reality doesn't erase the validity of transgender identities—it actually demonstrates that biological sex itself is more complex than simplistic claims suggest.
