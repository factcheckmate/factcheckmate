**Transgender and gender-diverse people have existed throughout human history and across all cultures.** Indigenous cultures worldwide have long recognized more than two genders: Two-Spirit people in many Native American cultures, Hijra in South Asia (recognized for over 4,000 years), Māhū in Hawaiian and Tahitian cultures, Fa'afafine in Samoa, and many others. Historical records document gender-diverse individuals in ancient Mesopotamia, classical Greece and Rome, and medieval Europe. The medical understanding and terminology has evolved, but the existence of people whose gender differs from their assigned sex is not new. What's new is greater visibility, medical knowledge, and—in some places—legal recognition and protection. The recent increase in people identifying as transgender reflects reduced stigma allowing people to live authentically, not a sudden emergence of a "new" phenomenon.
