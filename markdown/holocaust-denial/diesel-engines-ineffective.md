While diesel engines produce less carbon monoxide than gasoline engines, they still produce lethal concentrations in enclosed spaces, especially when engines are improperly tuned or run at specific settings. Nazi documents, survivor testimony, and perpetrator accounts confirm diesel engines were used at camps like Treblinka and Sobibor, and in gas vans. Additionally, some facilities used gasoline engines. The key factor is that any engine exhaust in a sealed chamber depletes oxygen while introducing toxic gases—multiple mechanisms cause death, not just carbon monoxide alone. Tests conducted by historians confirm diesel exhaust can kill within the timeframes described by witnesses.
