When scientists reviewed alleged chemtrail samples, 80-89% could be explained by common sources and poor sampling methods. Aluminum, barium, and strontium are abundant elements found naturally in soil, water, and air. Aluminosilicates are common in clay soils and minerals—measurements are dominated by ground-level sources, not aerial spraying. The 2016 survey of atmospheric experts found that faulty collection methods explain unusual results. For example, using Mason jars with metal lids contaminates samples, as does shaking the container. One expert noted: "The jar will contaminate the sample, as will the metal lid, particularly if you shake it." Legitimate air and soil sampling studies show no abnormal levels of heavy metals linked to aviation.
