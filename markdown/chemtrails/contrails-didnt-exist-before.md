Persistent contrails have been documented since World War II—long before chemtrail theories emerged. The UK Met Office states that "persistent contrails are not a new phenomenon" and notes extensive photo and film evidence from WWII combat aircraft showing the exact same formations. The High-Altitude Flight (later Meteorological Research Flight) was formed in 1942 specifically to investigate contrail formation conditions. The USAF analyzed contrail phenomena since at least 1953. The mechanism hasn't changed: hot engine exhaust meeting cold, humid air produces ice crystals. Contrails appear more common now due to massive increases in air travel—global flights increased from roughly 16 million in 1990 to over 40 million in 2019. More planes equals more contrails.
