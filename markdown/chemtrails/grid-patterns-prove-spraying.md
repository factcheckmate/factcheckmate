Grid patterns are the natural result of organized air traffic lanes, not evidence of spraying. Aircraft follow specific flight corridors managed by air traffic control, which creates crossing patterns—especially visible in busy airspace. The gridlike nature is particularly pronounced in countries with dense air traffic systems. From the ground, it's difficult to judge whether overlapping contrails are at similar altitudes or separated by thousands of feet. Wind shear and jet streams at cruising altitude can also distort contrails into unusual shapes. A 2000 multi-agency fact sheet from EPA, NASA, FAA, and NOAA specifically addressed this concern, explaining that flight lane organization naturally creates crosshatched patterns.
