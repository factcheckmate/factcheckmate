The only peer-reviewed papers claiming chemtrails exist were retracted for containing critical scientific errors. J. Marvin Herndon published two papers in 2015-2016 claiming to prove coal fly ash spraying, but both were retracted. The first contained a calculation error off by 2,000 times—the aluminum value he used was 70,000 μg/kg when the correct value was 140,000,000 μg/kg. This fundamental error invalidated all conclusions. The evidence consisted merely of showing element ratios in rainwater were similar to coal fly ash leachate—which proves nothing, as he never tested for actual coal fly ash or considered alternative explanations. The papers lacked scientific objectivity and relied on photos of normal contrails. In contrast, the 2016 survey of 77 atmospheric experts found zero credible evidence for chemtrails after reviewing the same types of claims.
