The NTP study has significant limitations that prevent it from proving cell phones cause cancer. Rats received whole-body radiation exposure for 9 hours daily over their lifetimes at levels up to 75 times higher than humans experience—conditions impossible to replicate in real-world cell phone use. The FDA formally disagreed with NTP's conclusions, noting the study wasn't designed to test cell phone safety in humans. Key issues include: only male rats showed effects, no clear dose-response relationship, exposed rats lived longer than controls, and the study used outdated 2G/3G technology. Most importantly, human data contradicts the findings—cell phone use skyrocketed from 62% to 96% of the US population (2002-2019) while brain cancer rates decreased.
